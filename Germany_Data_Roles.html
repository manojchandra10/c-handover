Your main route

EU Blue Card as a career starter

Needs
A degree recognised in Germany or comparable to a German one, a job that matches it, and pay of at least €45,934.20 a year for graduates of the last three years. The Federal Employment Agency approves the job at that rate. At €50,700 or more the standard rate applies.
You
Your September 2026 MSc makes you a career starter until about September 2029. Junior data scientist salaries in Berlin and Munich often clear the line.

For lower pay

Skilled-worker permit for graduates (§18b)

Needs
A recognised degree and an offer of qualified work. There is no set minimum pay under the age of 45. The Federal Employment Agency checks that pay and conditions match those of comparable German workers. Valid for up to four years.
You
Covers junior analyst jobs that pay under €45,934.20, which the Blue Card does not.

Needs a certificate and funds

Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte)

Needs
Up to a year in Germany to look for work. A fully recognised degree replaces the points test. You also need German at A1 or English at B2, and about €1,091 a month in funds (€13,092 for a year).
You
Possible once you hold an English certificate at B2 or above and the savings. A job offer first is the faster path.

How an application runs

  1. Look up the University of Nottingham and your MSc on anabin. An "H+" university with the degree marked "entspricht" (equivalent) needs nothing more. Otherwise apply to the ZAB for a statement of comparability, which a 2026 guide puts at €208 and two to three months.
  2. Get a job offer. Tell the employer you qualify for an EU Blue Card as a recent graduate.
  3. The employer can start the fast-track procedure at its local foreigners' office for €411, with your power of attorney. The Federal Employment Agency gives its approval inside that procedure.
  4. You hand in your national visa application at a TLScontact centre in London, Manchester or Edinburgh. Under the fast-track procedure the decision comes within three weeks.
  5. You enter Germany, register your address within two weeks of moving in, and collect the Blue Card from the foreigners' office.

Documents to gather

  • Passport and biometric photos.
  • BSc and MSc certificates and transcripts, with the anabin printouts or the ZAB statement. Ask the visa centre whether your Indian documents need any extra verification.
  • The signed contract or binding job offer, and the employer's declaration about the job.
  • Your CV.
  • Proof of health insurance from your start date.
  • The national visa fee, given as €75 in a 2026 guide.

Points to keep in mind

No German needed for the permit. Neither the Blue Card nor §18b asks for German. Some employers still do, which is why the roles below show the working language.

Pay figures change in January. The €45,934.20 and €50,700 lines are the 2026 values. An offer that is close to a line should be checked against the 2027 figures.

UK visa centres. German missions in the UK take applications from people who live lawfully in the UK, so keep your UK permission valid until your visa is issued.

Before you apply

The totals overstate the choice

Boards list thousands of "English-speaking" data jobs in Germany, but most are senior, and many junior adverts ask for German. Seven roles passed the checks in this round.

Ask about pay early

Your Blue Card line is €45,934.20 a year. Junior data analyst pay in Berlin, Munich and Hamburg runs at roughly €44,000 to €55,000 according to Join and Levels.fyi, so some offers will fall under it.

Check anabin this week

If your MSc is not shown as equivalent, apply to the ZAB at once. A statement takes about two to three months and could otherwise hold up a visa.

Mention the fast-track procedure

Employers can apply for you at their local foreigners' office for €411, which shortens the wait for a visa appointment and decision. Larger employers usually know it already.
