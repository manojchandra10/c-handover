Before you apply

Rolling deadlines close early

A rolling deadline means the employer reads applications as they arrive and takes the advert down once the role is filled. Apply within a few days of finding one.

Agency adverts

Many of these roles come through agencies such as GRB, Harnham, Adecco and Rullion. Ask who the employer is, and say you are applying for a Graduate visa, which needs no sponsorship from them.

Security vetting

Some consultancies place graduates on defence or government work that needs National Security Vetting. That often asks for several years of UK residence, so raise it before the assessment stage.

Training academies

Sigma Labs and EA Change train you and then place you with clients. Read the contract for minimum service periods and repayment clauses before you sign.

Adjustments at tests and interviews

You can ask any employer for adjustments, such as extra time, a short break or questions in advance. You do not have to explain your health in detail, and most forms have a box for it.

Underrated cities

These five have strong demand for data and tech work but get far less attention than the big ten. They are smaller than London or Manchester, so each advert is likely to draw fewer applicants. No dataset measures that directly.

Nottingham

3.3% of local jobs are in tech, joint third of 61 UK cities with Edinburgh and ahead of Manchester's 3.2%. Optisearch ranks it fifth for tech careers.

From home: you live here. Your Data Job Hunt 2026 tracker already holds the Nottingham roles, so none are repeated below.

Employers to watch: Experian, Capital One, Boots, E.ON Next, Nottingham University Hospitals and the University of Nottingham.

Derby

13th of UK cities for job adverts per resident (1,658 per 10,000), ahead of Cambridge. 2.3% of local jobs are in tech, the same share as Leeds.

From home: about 20 minutes by train.

Employers to watch: Derby City Council, which advertised a Business and Data Analyst at £39,000 to £40,000 in early October, plus Rolls-Royce, East Midlands Railway, Quick Release and Incora.

Leicester

2.7% of local jobs are in tech, slightly above London's 2.6%.

From home: about 30 minutes by train.

Employers to watch: NEXT, which had several analyst roles open in October, plus Dunelm, Cadent, Centrica and Hastings Direct.

Coventry

16th for job adverts per resident (1,398 per 10,000), ahead of Cambridge. Optisearch ranks it 11th for tech careers, with the third-highest average pay in its study.

From home: over an hour by train, so moving may suit better.

Employers to watch: Coventry City Council, Severn Trent and Coventry Building Society, with NESO and National Gas in nearby Warwick.

Milton Keynes

14th for job adverts per resident (1,558 per 10,000). 2.5% of local jobs are in tech, level with Glasgow, and Optisearch ranks it 12th.

From home: around two hours by train, so you would need to move.

Employers to watch: Santander, Network Rail, Red Bull Technology, Connells and Allica Bank.

York and Chester also do well on adverts per resident (15th and 5th), but no suitable data role was open there when checked. Junior-labelled data roles are scarcer in these smaller cities, so roles asking for one or two years of experience are worth applying for too.

Tech share of jobs comes from the Optisearch study (ONS data, January 2026). Adverts per resident come from a Davies & Associates study of Glassdoor listings (March 2026).
